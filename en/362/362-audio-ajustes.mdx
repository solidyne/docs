## Input Level

The Audimax 362HD **does not have manual input level control**.\
Adjustment is automatic via the **AGC** system.

- The rear panel features input connectors for **balanced (XLR and RJ45)** and **unbalanced (RCA)** equipment.
- It can be connected from non-professional consoles (–10 dBV) to broadcast consoles with balanced outputs at \+4 dBu or even \+8/\+12 dBu levels.
- The **AGC eliminates level variations** inherent in operation or recorded material:
  - If the level remains low, it compensates the signal to maintain uniform output.
  - If the level consistently exceeds 0 VU, it attenuates to prevent overload.

This way, the program signal reaches the processing stages at a constant level, achieving uniform on-air sound with consistent processing intensity.

The AGC (see section 2 image) features a three-stage indicator:

- The third LED (red) **should never remain permanently lit**.

## Sound Customization

The **Audimax 362HD** has been designed for easy and intuitive operation. No specialized audio processing knowledge is required to set it up and adjust its sound.

Initial setup: simply set all controls to center position... and you're done! \
You'll be on-air with proper sound quality.

Sound customization is achieved through **seven rotary controls** on the front panel.

### Dynamic Equalizer

The 3-band dynamic equalizer acts on the **compression thresholds** of each band.\
Unlike a conventional equalizer, **its effect increases with higher signal levels**.

- Ensure the multiband compressor **is engaged** ("Multiband compressor" LEDs lit).
- Adjust the _Dynamic Equalizer_ knobs for **low, mid, and high frequencies**:
  - Turn left → increases compression in that band.
  - Turn right → decreases compression, freeing the band and boosting those frequencies.

<Note>
  Maintain a balanced relationship between the three bands. Extreme differences create imbalance between lows, mids, and highs.
</Note>

- To achieve impact in low frequencies, slightly free this band compared to mids and highs.
- Remember listeners may boost bass on their receivers; therefore, deliver clean low frequencies without over-compression.

### Band Energy

These controls **increase peak density in the bands**, modifying the **release times** of the compressor.

- To the right → **compact and aggressive** sound (fast release).
- To the left → **smooth and more dynamic** sound (slow release).

### Multiband Compressor

It is the heart of the processing. The indicators show the action of the **band compressors**.

  src="/images/362/362-frente-bandas.png"

The **Multiband** control adjusts the input gain to the compressors.\
It is the control that most impacts the **sound of the radio**.

- To the left → soft sound, less processed, with lower energy.
- To the right → strong sound, with more energy, but with less dynamics and less naturalness.

<Warning>
  Excessive processing can result in a very loud but **flattened** sound (with no dynamics) and unnatural voices.
</Warning>

<Info>
  A proper working level is achieved when the multiband compressor indicators act without reaching maximum illumination.
</Info>

- For natural and balanced voices: middle or lower position of the Multiband control.
- For more intense processing: use **slow recovery times** (Band Energy to the left).

## Relationship Between Controls

For **Dynamic EQ** and **Band Energy** to function, the **multiband compressor must be active**. This means that in each band, at least the first LEDs must be lit.\
Without multiband compression, the controls have no effect, as they are associated with the compression process.
